Research project · 2022 — 2024

PoliS — Poli-Stability: a comprehensive assessment of stability in pollination communities

Bartomeus Lab, Estación Biológica de Doñana Funded by the MSCA programme, European Commission

Species and their ecological interactions are disappearing faster than ever, and one overlooked side of that loss is what happens to pollination — an ecosystem function human wellbeing depends on. PoliS asked how plant-pollinator communities respond to environmental change, bridging the usual divide between empirical and theoretical approaches to ecological stability.

The project drew on seven years of detailed data from twelve pollinator communities across a landscape-fragmentation gradient in the Doñana natural reserve, aiming to quantify how these communities respond to environmental perturbations and to uncover the mechanisms that let them withstand global-change pressures.

Structure

WP1 — Characterise and quantify the stability of the empirical communities under study.

WP2 — Validate mechanistic approaches: measure stability in model plant-pollinator communities, then compare it against the empirical communities.

WP3 — Interaction structure and its consequences for stability, studied through the effect of landscape fragmentation and through the dynamical nature of the interactions themselves.

Outcomes

Fieldwork included public engagement, among it an invertebrate bioblitz in Tablada, Seville. Results compared the theoretical expectation for pollinator persistence against the values observed across the twelve field sites, and looked at how persistence probability relates to the number of years each pollinator species was recorded at a site.
